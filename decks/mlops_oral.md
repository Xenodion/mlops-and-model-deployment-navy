# From a scooter ride to a recoverable model release

Team NAVY · MLOps and Model Deployment · 10-minute presentation followed by 10 minutes of questions

An operator following one ride gives the jury a thread through the system. The same ride can connect the data, registry, API, front end and release checks. The operator is a fictional use case. The data, versions and demonstrations can be traced to the repository. Times below are a rehearsal budget. A teammate can substitute a screenshot, a short recording or a live view according to what reads best in the room.

| Time | Slide and question | Content and possible evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:40 | 1 · What does an operator get? | A ride category across five classes and a distinct fall-risk score from recent motion. In live mode the category accumulates across completed windows. Post-ride mode supports retrospective review. A real ride with two aligned tracks could establish the use case without claiming prevented accidents. |
| 0:40–1:40 | 2 · Could another teammate reproduce the pipeline? | Trace the GBG500 and SRV3490 sources through acquisition, DVC data versions, split, preprocessing, training, evaluation and registration. Name the actual download source and any Kaggle step used. Explain where the team stores private data and model artifacts, including the S3-compatible store. A DVC DAG beside a commit and data hash could address reproducibility more directly than a list of tools. |
| 1:40–2:40 | 3 · Which model is good enough to release? | Follow one MLflow run to its metrics, artifacts, numeric version and production alias. Give the ride classifier its golden ride-level macro-F1 and the fall detector its held-out group metric, gap and label definition. The b80aeeb API reproduced macro-F1 0.51073 on 100 rides. The saved 5 s gap scores gave paired AUC 0.888 over 205 evaluable groups. Fresh artifact scoring would strengthen the latter. A registry screenshot paired with a small evaluation table could explain the promotion gate and why two metrics are needed. |
| 2:40–4:10 | 4 · Can someone actually use it? | Replay a known ride, then upload or review the same ride after completion. Point to category and fall risk separately, show what state the live client sends back and why the post-ride risk is retrospective. The two modes both returned the expected fields in the b80aeeb replay. A side-by-side web app and Swagger example, or a recorded demo, could demonstrate usability and API functionality. |
| 4:10–5:20 | 5 · How does a change reach production? | Follow one PR through unit, integration and end-to-end tests, evaluation gate, code-only Docker image, manual approval, then the self-hosted runner deploying to kind. Distinguish Compose development from the local Kubernetes production environment. Show how the image tag and two model versions identify the release. A real Actions run plus the approval or stable deployment is evidence for both automation and collaboration. |
| 5:20–6:15 | 6 · What load fits the latency budget? | Trace one versus multiple replicas with identical offered traffic. Read completed requests/s, p95, error rate and resource limits together. Distinguish the provisional 200 ms live target from measured p95 and the separate post-ride target. A Locust load curve or a small table of the last passing and first failing load would make replica choice understandable. Use the Cloud capacity evidence when the workloads match. |
| 6:15–7:15 | 7 · How would we notice a bad release? | Walk through infrastructure, input, prediction and delayed outcome monitoring. For each, give one actionable signal, its source and the first person or runbook to consult. A Grafana crop with a correlated structured log can show which layer first detected an incident. Delayed labels constrain claims about live quality. |
| 7:15–8:05 | 8 · Would drift justify retraining? | Use the sensor-unit scenario to distinguish an upstream data repair from a new training run. Explain the reference window, sample size and threshold for the selected continuous or categorical drift test. K–S, chi-squared, PSI or an Evidently report may support different parts of the diagnosis. Show the actual configured test and a documented trigger. A before/after sensor distribution plus workflow history could connect drift, investigation, retraining and the gate. |
| 8:05–9:10 | 9 · How quickly can we withdraw a candidate? | A bounded canary exercises recovery with limited exposure. A/B would need a product outcome and comparable cohorts. Shadowing would duplicate compute and needs a risk that replay cannot cover. State the proposed p95 and error rules, minimum traffic, manual decision and known-good image/model pair. A timestamped incident timeline could show withdrawal, disappearance of candidate traffic and verification of both stable outputs. Under five minutes is the drill target until timing evidence exists. |
| 9:10–10:00 | 10 · What would it take to put this into service? | Proposed pilot sequence: historical replay, operator review of alert usefulness, controlled exposure with a named operator, then expansion if quality, load and recovery criteria hold. Explain who owns model/API changes, infrastructure, promotion and incident response. One release checklist or three pilot milestones could close the story. Credit contributors via their PRs and identify the next approval. |
